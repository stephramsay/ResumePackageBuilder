# Voice and Story Selection

Stephanie's interview voice should feel:

- Precise and senior.
- Conversational and natural.
- Warm, empathetic, kind, and caring.
- Specific about examples and tradeoffs.
- Plainspoken rather than inflated.

Avoid:

- Corporate over-polish.
- Huge words where ordinary words work.
- Over-explaining metrics.
- Inventing certainty about a company strategy.
- Rewriting memorized core narratives unless the role requires a targeted bridge.

Story selection priority:

1. Best fit to likely role question.
2. Strongest approved metric or concrete outcome.
3. Cleanest role/company bridge.
4. Distinct angle from other routes.
5. Backup story available if interviewer pushes for a different example.

When two stories share the same broad project, choose the route based on angle:

- Same project plus different stakeholder, conflict, metric, lesson, or product surface can be a meaningful variation.
- Same project plus same situation/action/result/lesson is a duplicate and should not become a new route unless the wording is needed as a backup cue.

Fixed behavioral mapping:

- For "Tell me about a time you had to push back on a stakeholder request that was not aligned with product strategy," use `STORY_03_HEALTH_SUMMARY_REPORT` with the provider-portal request angle.
- The answer should frame sales asking for a provider portal, Stephanie diagnosing the real need, avoiding a one-off portal that would distract from strategy, and steering toward the Health Summary Report as a simpler, more accessible, more reusable way to prove value to providers and close the between-visit communication gap.
- Do not map this question to the broad platform rebuild, platform transition weakness, coworker conflict, or mobile technical-debt story unless the interviewer specifically asks for those angles.
- Keep the tone constructive: not "I said no," but "I understood the need and offered a more strategic path."

Framework selection priority:

1. Match the role's likely product judgment demands.
2. Choose frameworks Stephanie can naturally return to under pressure.
3. Prefer distinct lenses over overlapping frameworks.
4. Keep the framework cue short enough for Cluely to surface quickly.
5. Pair frameworks with stories whenever possible.

Frameworks should make Stephanie sound calm and senior, not canned. Use them for structure:

- "I would start with the workflow..."
- "I would look for the highest-leverage constraint..."
- "I would adjust the experiment based on risk..."
- "I would define AI's role in the workflow first..."

Avoid:

- Turning framework-bank text into generic interview answers.
- Adding frameworks where a specific story already answers the question cleanly.
- Citing a framework as evidence. It is a thinking scaffold, not a source fact about Stephanie's experience.
